2 marks2. Features of Coordination (Ch 1 · asked 3 times)

Coordination is the process by which a manager synchronises the activities of different departments to achieve a common goal. It is the essence of management.

For 2 marks, write any 2 of these 6:

  1. Integrates group efforts into purposeful work.
  2. Ensures unity of action between departments.
  3. Is a continuous process: from planning to controlling.
  4. Is all pervasive: needed at all levels and in all departments.
  5. Is the responsibility of all managers: top, middle and lower.
  6. Is a deliberate function: done consciously by managers.
🧠 "I Use a C-A-R-D": Integrates, Unity, Continuous, All pervasive, Responsibility of all, Deliberate. Full topic: Tier 1 → Coordination.

1–2 marks3. Leadership Styles (Ch 7 · asked 3 times)

Leadership is the process of influencing the behaviour of people by making them strive voluntarily towards achieving organisational goals.
StyleHow the leader behavesKey words to spot
Autocratic / AuthoritarianGives orders and expects subordinates to obey. Takes decisions alone. One-way communication. Allows quick decision making."decides alone", "obey", "no discussion"
Democratic / ParticipativeMakes plans and decisions in consultation with subordinates. Encourages participation, respects their opinions and is supportive."consults", "takes suggestions", "team decides with manager"
Laissez-faire / Free-reinDoes not use power unless absolutely essential. Followers get a high degree of independence to set their own goals and methods. The manager is there only to support them."complete freedom", "sets own goals", "manager only helps"
📌 Case lines
  • "The manager decides everything and wants orders followed without questions" → Autocratic
  • "The manager discusses with the team before deciding" → Democratic
  • "The research team sets its own goals; the manager only provides resources" → Laissez-faire
✍️ Name any one style of leadership. (1 mark) Democratic (participative) leadership. Autocratic and laissez-faire are also correct.

1–2 marks4. Demonetisation and LPG (Ch 3 · match-the-following 3 times)

Demonetisation: On 8 November 2016, the Government of India announced that the two largest notes, ₹500 and ₹1,000, would stop being legal tender with immediate effect. This made 86% of the money in circulation invalid.

Aim: to curb corruption, counterfeiting, the use of high-value notes for illegal activities, and especially black money (income not declared to tax authorities).

Features of demonetisation

  1. A tax administration measure: those with black money had to declare it and pay tax at a penalty rate.
  2. A signal that tax evasion will no longer be tolerated.
  3. Channels savings into the formal financial system: new bank deposits can support loans at lower interest rates.
  4. Creates a less-cash (cash-lite) economy: more digital transactions and better tax compliance.

Impact of demonetisation

AreaImpact
Money / interest ratesFewer cash transactions, more bank deposits, more financial savings
Private wealthDeclined (some notes not returned; real estate prices fell)
Public sector wealthNo effect
DigitisationDigital transactions by new users (RuPay/AEPS) increased
Real estatePrices declined
Tax collectionIncome tax collection rose because of more disclosure

LPG: New Industrial Policy, July 1991

  • Liberalisation: freeing Indian business and industry from unnecessary controls and restrictions (end of the licence-permit-quota raj).
  • Privatisation: giving the private sector a greater role and reducing the role of the public sector, e.g. through disinvestment.
  • Globalisation: the integration of the economies of the world into one cohesive global economy.
✍️ State the impact of demonetisation on interest rates, private wealth and real estate. Interest rates: more bank deposits and financial savings (which supports lower interest rates on loans). Private wealth: declined. Real estate: prices declined.
✍️ What is disinvestment? (1 mark) Transfer of public sector enterprises to the private sector. It reduces the government's stake. If government ownership falls below 51%, ownership and management pass to the private sector.

1–2 marks10. Types of Plans (Ch 4)

Single-use vs Standing plans

BasisSingle-use PlanStanding Plan
MeaningMade for a one-time event or projectUsed for activities that occur regularly
SituationNon-recurringRecurring (routine decisions)
IncludesBudgets, programmes, projectsPolicies, procedures, methods, rules
LifeEnds when the event or project endsDeveloped once, modified from time to time

The 8 types of plans

  1. Objective: the end result the management wants to reach. E.g. increase sales by 10%.
  2. Strategy: a comprehensive plan for achieving objectives. It has 3 dimensions: (i) determining long-term objectives, (ii) adopting a course of action, (iii) allocating resources.
  3. Policy: a general statement that guides thinking and decisions. E.g. recruitment policy, pricing policy, admission policy.
  4. Procedure: routine steps, in chronological order, for carrying out an activity. E.g. procedure for requisitioning supplies.
  5. Method: the prescribed way or manner of doing one task (one step of a procedure). E.g. the training method chosen for each level.
  6. Rule: a specific statement of what must or must not be done. No flexibility or discretion. The simplest plan. E.g. report for work at 9 a.m.
  7. Programme: a detailed statement about a project, covering objectives, policies, procedures, rules, tasks, resources and budget.
  8. Budget: a statement of expected results in numbers. It is also a control device. E.g. sales budget, cash budget.
🧠 "Our School Principal Prefers Morning Rules, Prayers & Books": Objective, Strategy, Policy, Procedure, Method, Rule, Programme, Budget
📌 Case lines
  • "Rama Stationery Mart decided to make all payments by e-transfer only" → Rule (standing plan)
  • "Airtel doubled data on its ₹149 plan to fight Jio" → Strategy
  • "Mitticool keeps all products at prices the poor can afford" → Policy
  • "A plan that also serves as a controlling device" → Budget (single-use)
  • "A plan concerned with how a physical or technical task is done" → Method (standing)
✍️ Why are rules considered to be plans? (1 mark) Because they are decided in advance and guide future action by telling exactly what must or must not be done.

1–2 marks11. Barriers to Communication (Ch 7)

1. Semantic barriers (language problems)

  • Badly expressed message
  • Symbols with different meanings
  • Faulty translations
  • Unclarified assumptions
  • Technical jargon
  • Body language and gesture decoding

2. Psychological / emotional barriers

  • Premature evaluation
  • Lack of attention
  • Loss by transmission and poor retention
  • Distrust

3. Organisational barriers

  • Organisational policy
  • Rules and regulations
  • Status
  • Complexity in organisation structure
  • Organisational facilities

4. Personal barriers

  • Fear of challenge to authority
  • Lack of confidence of the superior in subordinates
  • Unwillingness to communicate
  • Lack of proper incentives
🧠 "S-P-O-P": Semantic, Psychological, Organisational, Personal. The ways to remove these barriers are on Tier 1 → Improving communication.
📌 Case lines
  • "The manager used technical terms the workers did not understand" → Semantic (technical jargon)
  • "The boss judged the idea before hearing it fully" → Psychological (premature evaluation)
  • "The message passed through too many levels and got distorted" → Organisational (complex structure)
  • "The superior hides information, fearing loss of authority" → Personal (fear of challenge to authority)

1–2 marks12. Staffing Terms (Ch 6)

TermMeaning (1 mark)
Job rotationShifting a trainee from one department or job to another, to give a broad understanding of the business.
OrientationIntroducing a new employee to superiors, colleagues and subordinates, and familiarising him with the rules and policies.
ApprenticeshipOn-the-job training where the trainee works under a master worker to gain a high level of skill (e.g. plumbers, electricians).
Transfer (internal source)Shifting an employee from one job, department or shift to another, without a substantive change in responsibility or status.
Promotion (internal source)Shifting an employee to a higher position with more responsibility, status and pay.

Merits of internal sources

  • Employees are motivated by the chance of promotion
  • Selection and placement are simpler (the candidate is already known)
  • Transfer works as a tool of training
  • Moves staff from surplus to deficient departments
  • Cheaper than external recruitment

Limitations of internal sources

  • No fresh talent enters the organisation
  • Employees may become lethargic, expecting promotion anyway
  • A new enterprise cannot use them
  • The spirit of competition among employees is hampered
  • Frequent transfers may reduce productivity

Full topics: Tier 1 → Training methods · Tier 1 → Staffing process

✍️ Name the two internal sources of recruitment. (1 mark) Transfers and promotions.

1 mark13. Quick-Fact Bank

Critical Point Control: Control should focus on key result areas (KRAs), which are critical to the success of the organisation. E.g. a 5% rise in labour cost matters more than a 15% rise in postal charges.
Management by Exception: "An attempt to control everything results in controlling nothing." Only significant deviations beyond the permissible limit are brought to management's notice.
Advantages of both: saves managers' time, focuses attention on important areas, leaves routine problems to subordinates (which helps delegation and morale), and identifies critical problems in time.
